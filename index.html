How to read this page

Written for someone who does not follow football. If you read one thing on a game, read "In plain words."

What the app does

Every day it reads what the betting market thinks of every game, writes it down in plain words, freezes that at kickoff, and after the game scores everyone who made a call: the market, the app's own numbers, the CBS writers, and each person who picked here. The point is not to predict games. The point is that after a few weeks you know who is worth listening to.

Inside the matchup panel

FieldPlain meaning
The bar, for example ATL 37% / PIT 63%How often each side wins if the game were played many times. Each team's share sits under its own name.
In plain wordsA few sentences that say everything below without the vocabulary.
Line, the head startThe market's score prediction. "PIT by 3.5" means Pittsburgh is expected to finish 3.5 points ahead.
Total, points both teams togetherHow many points the market expects in the whole game. High means a shootout, low means a slog.
Market win probabilityThe market's own answer to "who wins," taken from the price of the win bet with the book's fee removed.
Forecast sourceWhether the number came from the market line or, before a line exists, from team strength alone.
Rolling team stateA small nudge for how a team has done against expectations this season. Zero until games are played.
Rest differenceWho had more days off since their last game. Worth a fraction of a point.
ConditionsThe weather at the stadium at kickoff.
Win board side-by-sideEach team's expected wins for the year, market versus app, and what moved it.
Communication continuityInjuries grouped by the units that have to talk to each other on the field. A lower score means more disruption.
Preparation disruptionMissed practices and roster status this week.
Outside picksWhat each CBS writer and their consensus took, straight up and against the spread.
Rating modelThe app's second opinion built from last season's plays, with the named starting quarterbacks. Shown and graded, never used in the forecast, because it did not beat the market on the 2025 test.
Projected startersWho starts at quarterback, per ESPN's depth chart, refreshed every run.
Player watchNotable injuries per team with their status.
Your pick, Picking asChoose your name, tap a winner and, if you want, a spread side, then save the week once. Your latest save before each kickoff counts.
Late-news adjustmentA slider for something you know that the line has not absorbed. It changes your screen only, never the ledger.

Two words you will see everywhere

WordPlain meaning
Winner, or straight upWho wins the game, by any score.
Spread, or against the spreadWho beats the head start. Take Pittsburgh at 3.5 and they must win by 4 or more. Take Atlanta and they can lose by 3 and you still win.

The scorecard, once games are graded

FieldPlain meaning
GradedGames with a final score that had a forecast frozen before kickoff.
Model accuracy, Market accuracyThe share of games each one called right, straight up.
BrierHow far the confidence was from what happened. Lower is better. Saying 63% and losing hurts less than saying 90% and losing.
EdgeModel Brier minus market Brier. Negative means the app beat the market. Expect near zero.
Per person columnsThe same grades for each picker, straight up and against the spread, next to the CBS writers.
